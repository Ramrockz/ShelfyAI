The problem

Stock only ever tells the truth on the day you count it.

You start a batch and can't finish it.One part is gone and the customer already has a date.
You buy what you already had.Three boxes of it, on the shelf you didn't check.
You price on last year's costs.Materials went up, the price didn't, and the margin quietly went to zero.
The update never happens.Keeping the sheet current is a second job, and it loses to the first one every week.

Every one of those comes from the same place: nothing changes unless you type it in.

How it works

Type it in once. After that, send paper.

1
Count what's there today

Add your materials and what you have of each. This is the only manual entry in the whole thing.

2
Photograph the receipt, paste the order

A photo, a PDF or a link from your shop. Every line comes back read and matched to items you already have.

3
Check it before it saves

You see the quantities and prices it found and correct anything wrong. Nothing moves behind your back.

4
The count keeps itself

Purchases add stock, orders take it away, and the cost of everything you make follows along.

What changes

Three questions you stop guessing at.

"Can I make this today?"

Each product shows how many you could build right now from what's actually on the shelf, and which material is stopping you.

"What do I need to order?"

Anything below the minimum you set surfaces at the top of the list, before it becomes a customer problem.

"Am I making money on this?"

Cost per product is rebuilt from the last price you actually paid for each material, not the price you remember.
